Relay Brokers and Barley Flows in the Umma Province,
Ur III Period (ca. 2112–2004 BCE)

A Computational Analysis of ~940 CDLI Tablets

Asa Rodan Ur III Grain Economy Pipeline Project
Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative (CDLI) Source Corpus
Submitted: June 2026

Keywords: Ur III · Barley distribution · Administrative tablets · Computational Assyriology · Network analysis · Umma · Grain economy · ATF parsing

Abstract This study presents a computational analysis of 940 cuneiform administrative tablets from the Ur III Umma provincial archive (ca. 2112–2004 BCE), sourced from the Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative (CDLI) corpus. An automated ATF-parsing pipeline extracted 1,270 commodity transactions, of which 925 (72.8%) involved barley (še). Bilateral completeness — transactions recording both an issuing party and a receiving party — reached 27.1% (251 of 925). Quantity analysis reveals a median transfer of 900 sila₃ (~900 litres) with significant right skew driven by annual provincial summaries denominated in šar₂ units. Actor-network analysis identifies 345 named nodes, 54 of whom function as relay brokers mediating between institutional sources and downstream consumers. Campaign-dated tablets (n=86) exhibit a markedly higher bilateral completion rate (42%) than the overall corpus (27%), suggesting more rigorous double-entry recording during administrative periods of military mobilisation. A targeted search for military-logistics entries found zero attestations of aga₃-us₂ (soldier/gendarme) or erin₂ (troop levy) as transaction parties, indicating that this specific archive does not directly support a grain-to-military-logistics argument. The relay-broker hypothesis is partially supported by the structural network data but complicated by the asymmetric flow profile of the highest-volume node (Lu-duga). Methodological limitations, including unvalidated extraction precision/recall and partial bilateral coverage, are disclosed throughout.

1. Introduction

The Ur III state (ca. 2112–2004 BCE) produced one of the ancient world's most bureaucratically dense archive systems. Thousands of clay tablets recording grain disbursements, labour rations, and commodity transfers survive from provincial centres — particularly Umma, Girsu/Lagaš, and the central redistribution depot at Drehem (Puzriš-Dagan). These texts, written in Sumerian using a standardised cuneiform script, offer unparalleled resolution for studying pre-monetary redistributive economies.

Traditional philological study of these tablets proceeds tablet-by-tablet, privileging linguistic precision over aggregate pattern-detection. While foundational editions such as those of Snell (1982) and Maekawa (1989) on the Umma corpus established robust frameworks for individual text interpretation, the sheer volume of the surviving corpus (~100,000 tablets in public collections) resists comprehensive manual synthesis. Computational approaches, first systematically deployed through the CDLI project (Englund & Grégoire 1991; Englund 2011), have opened the possibility of corpus-level quantitative analysis.

This paper reports the findings of an automated extraction pipeline applied to approximately 940 CDLI-encoded tablets from the Umma provincial archive. The primary research questions are:

  1. What is the structure of barley-flow networks in the Umma archive, and do named intermediaries function as relay brokers between institutional sources and consumers?
  2. Does the bilateral record rate vary systematically with reign period, season, or administrative context?
  3. Does the corpus contain evidence of direct grain distribution to military personnel or units?

The Umma province was chosen because its archive is one of the largest and most consistently encoded in the CDLI corpus, and because prior scholarship (e.g., Steinkeller 2003; Garfinkle 2012) has proposed it as a key node in the Ur III inter-provincial grain economy.

2. Materials and Methods

2.1 Source Corpus

All tablets were downloaded from the CDLI public corpus in ASCII Transliteration Format (ATF), a standardised encoding developed by Englund and Tinney for machine- readable representation of cuneiform sign sequences. The subcorpus targeted tablets tagged with the Umma provenance and dated to the Ur III period. After filtering incomplete stubs (tablets with fewer than three parseable lines), the working corpus comprised 940 tablets.

2.2 ATF Parsing Pipeline

A purpose-built Python pipeline (atf_pipeline.py) was developed to extract structured transaction records from raw ATF text. The pipeline applies the following sequential operations:

  1. Quantity detection — regex patterns recognise numeric sequences followed by capacity unit terms (sila₃, ban₂, barig, šar₂, gin₂) and convert to a normalised sila₃ base unit (1 barig = 60 sila₃; 1 šar₂ = 1,080,000 sila₃).
  2. Commodity identification — keyword matching against a curated list of Sumerian commodity terms (še = barley, nig₂-àr-ra = flour, kaš = beer, ì = oil, udu = sheep).
  3. Actor extraction — heuristic pattern matching identifies candidate issuer tokens following ki (ablative; "from") and giri₃ (via), and recipient tokens following šu ba-ti ("received") or ba-zi ("disbursed to").
  4. Name normalisation — extracted tokens undergo cleaning: removal of damage notation (!?*#), partial-restoration brackets ([…]), sign-variant parentheticals ((ba) in nig₂-lagar(ba)), terminative suffix -še₃, ablative suffix -ta, the Sumerian conjunction u₃ ("and") when prepended to names, and occupational titles from a controlled vocabulary of 18 attested terms (e.g., dub-sar, nu-banda₃, muḥaldim).
  5. Date extraction — regnal-year patterns (mu PN lugal-e …) are matched against a reference table of Ur III kings and year names; campaign references (ki-maš, ḫarši, Lullubi, Simurrum) are flagged for sub-corpus analysis.

2.3 Bilateral Completeness and Limitations

A transaction is classified bilateral when both an issuer and a recipient are successfully extracted, issuer-only or recipient-only when only one party is recovered, and neither when the heuristics find no named party. The current pipeline has not been validated against a manually-curated gold standard; extraction precision and recall are therefore unknown. The 27% bilateral rate is a lower bound on true bilateral content in the corpus: many administrative tablets are single-entry receipts where the absent party is institutional context rather than a named person.

Methodological Caveat Quantities denominated in šar₂ (1 šar₂ = 1,080,000 sila₃) represent annual provincial summaries, not individual commodity transfers. Aggregate volume statistics that include šar₂-unit records are arithmetically correct but analytically misleading. All volume comparisons in this paper are restricted to records denominated in sila₃, ban₂, or barig unless explicitly noted.

2.4 Network Construction

A directed bipartite network was constructed in which nodes represent named actors (individuals, institutions, or generic roles) and directed edges represent attested barley transfers. Edge weight equals the total sila₃ volume transferred across all attested transactions between a given source–target pair. A node is classified as a relay broker if it appears as both issuer and recipient across the corpus and its out-degree exceeds its in-degree by a factor of at least 1.5.

3.3 Temporal Patterns

Reign Period

Of 925 barley transactions, 588 (63.6%) carry a legible regnal-year date. Distribution across Ur III kings reflects both archive depth and scribal practice:

The dominance of Šulgi-reign tablets is consistent with the peak of Ur III administrative centralisation during his long 48-year reign, during which the province-level redistribution system reached its most elaborate form (Steinkeller 1987; Maekawa 1989).

Seasonal Distribution

The month sze-sag₁₁-ku₅ (Barley Harvest, month I in the Umma calendar) leads with 39 transactions — the single most active month in the dataset. The secondary peak falls in months VII–VIII (du₆-ku₃ / apin-du₈-a), corresponding to the autumn sowing preparation period. This bimodal pattern mirrors the dual-season agricultural cycle attested in Ur III agronomic lexica (Waetzoldt 1987).

Campaign-Dated Tablets

Eighty-six tablets carry year names referencing military campaigns (mu ki-maš ba-ḫul, mu ḫar-ši ba-ḫul, and related formulae from Šulgi's eastern campaigns). These tablets exhibit a bilateral completion rate of 42% (36/86), compared to 27% for the corpus overall — a statistically notable differential suggesting that campaign-year tablets were subject to more rigorous double-entry recording, possibly reflecting heightened central oversight during periods of military mobilisation.

Finding: Campaign-Year Documentation Effect Campaign-year tablets are 55% more likely to carry complete bilateral records than the overall corpus (42% vs 27%). This may reflect administrative discipline imposed by the logistical demands of military campaigns, requiring tighter accountability chains — or it may reflect a selection effect if campaign-year tablets were preferentially preserved by virtue of their official significance.

The Šara-temple appearing prominently in both the issuer and recipient lists is consistent with its role as Umma's paramount temple estate, functioning both as a grain disburser (paying rations to attached personnel) and as a grain receiver (accepting offerings and inter-institutional transfers). Recorded volume received by the Šara-temple across bilateral records totals approximately 87,996 sila₃ (~88,000 litres), implying a sustained monthly inflow well above what would be needed for incense or sacrifice alone, and suggesting support for attached personnel and workshops.

3.5 Network Topology and the Relay Broker Structure

The full transaction network contains 345 nodes and approximately 420 directed edges (bilateral records only). The degree distribution is highly skewed: the top 10 nodes account for over 60% of all attested transaction volume. Fifty-four nodes function as relay brokers by the criterion defined in §2.4 — appearing as both issuers and recipients with out-degree substantially exceeding in-degree.

Finding: Relay Broker Cohort Fifty-four named actors bridge institutional issuers (temples, storehouses, processing facilities) and downstream named recipients, consistent with Garfinkle's (2012) model of mid-level administrators serving as human conduits in the Ur III redistributive system.

The highest-volume named actor, Lu-duga, presents an interesting anomaly within the relay model: he appears as issuer in 49 transactions but as recipient in only 2, producing an extreme asymmetry (out:in ratio ≈ 24.5:1) that exceeds the relay pattern and suggests a role closer to that of a terminal distributor or institutional output agent. His volume issued in sila₃ units alone substantially exceeds that of any other named individual in the dataset.

Negative Finding: No Military Logistics Signal This corpus does not contain evidence of direct grain disbursement to identified military personnel or units. Campaign toponyms appear exclusively in year-name formulae (calendar references), not as transaction parties or destination labels. The one identified soldier/guard-adjacent transaction records 120 sila₃ — a single monthly ration equivalent consistent with incidental administrative recording rather than organised logistics. Researchers seeking evidence of Ur III grain-to-military logistics should consult the Drehem (Puzriš-Dagan) archive, which served as the centralised depot for inter-provincial resource allocation during military campaigns.

4. Discussion

4.1 The Relay Hypothesis: Partial Support

The network data partially supports a relay hypothesis: a stable cohort of 54 named broker officials mediates between large institutional sources and named downstream consumers. This is consistent with the administrative sociology described by Garfinkle (2012) and with the hierarchical authority chains documented by Steinkeller (2003) for the Umma provincial administration. The giri₃ ("via") formulation in many tablets further attests to named intermediary accountability — a conveyor of record, not merely a physical carrier.

However, the relay model as applied to this corpus faces two complications. First, bilateral coverage is only 27%, meaning the network is substantially incomplete: many transfer edges are one-directional in the record even if both parties were tracked in the original administration. Second, Lu-duga's profile — dominant issuer, near-absent recipient — may represent a genuine exception to the relay pattern, or it may reflect archival accident (his inflow receipts are simply not in this subcorpus). Resolving this would require cross-referencing with complementary tablet collections from Umma that are not included in the current pipeline run.

4.2 The Bilateral Threshold Effect

Bilateral transactions have a median quantity approximately 33% higher than issuer-only transactions (1,200 vs 900 sila₃). One interpretation is that larger transfers required more formal documentation, triggering the generation of both outgoing and incoming receipts. An alternative explanation is that bilateral tablets represent a specific document type — perhaps inter-institutional transfers requiring countersignature — that was independently administered from single-party ration records. The current data cannot distinguish between these interpretations. A genre-classification step applied to the raw ATF (distinguishing ration lists from balanced accounts from receipts) would be necessary to adjudicate this question.

4.3 Seasonality and the Agricultural Calendar

The peak in sze-sag₁₁-ku₅ (Barley Harvest month) and a secondary peak in months VII–VIII is consistent with the Umma agricultural calendar as reconstructed by Waetzoldt (1987) and Maekawa (1984). The harvest-month peak is straightforward: newly harvested grain enters the redistribution system and triggers a burst of administrative recording. The autumn secondary peak plausibly reflects grain release for sowing, a pattern well-attested in Ur III agronomic texts from Girsu (Englund 1991). The seasonality signal in this computational corpus thus aligns with findings from traditional philological study — lending modest support to the pipeline's validity.

4.4 The šar₂ Classification Problem

Four tablets in the corpus carry quantities in excess of 2,000,000 sila₃, all denominated in šar₂ units. Cross-referencing with the ATF source confirms these are annual provincial summaries (tablets of the nig₂-šid / balanced-account genre), not individual transfers. Including them in volume aggregates produces a mean that is meaningless for economic analysis: the "mean transfer" of 26,976 sila₃ (~27,000 litres) is over 30 times the actual median of 900 sila₃. Future iterations of the pipeline should implement automatic genre detection to exclude summary accounts from distributional statistics, or at minimum apply a unit-type filter.

4.5 Comparison to Previous Scholarship

The structural findings of this study broadly corroborate the picture of the Umma provincial economy drawn by Maekawa (1989): a temple-centred redistributive system in which named mid-level administrators serve as accountable conduits between institutional stores and consuming units. The prominence of the Šara-temple as both issuer and recipient aligns with Maekawa's identification of the Šara temple estate as Umma's dominant institutional actor. The absence of military logistics entries in the Umma archive also accords with Steinkeller's (2003) argument that the Drehem archive, not provincial archives, handled inter-institutional resource flows for military provisioning.

Where this study diverges from prior work is in scale and method. By analysing ~940 tablets computationally rather than reading 20–30 key texts in depth, it gains aggregate distributional data unavailable to traditional philological approaches while sacrificing per-tablet interpretive depth. The two approaches are complementary rather than competing.

5. Limitations and Future Work

Limitations Summary
  1. Unvalidated precision/recall. No gold-standard comparison set has been compiled. The pipeline may systematically miss certain name patterns or extract spurious tokens for others.
  2. 27% bilateral coverage. Three-quarters of barley transactions yield at most one named party. Network-level conclusions are drawn from a structurally incomplete graph.
  3. šar₂ contamination. Annual summary records inflate aggregate volume statistics and must be filtered for economic analysis.
  4. No genre classification. Ration lists, balanced accounts, and receipts are not distinguished; the dataset is a genre mixture.
  5. Subcorpus selection bias. Only CDLI-encoded tablets with at least minimal ATF markup are included. Tablets not yet encoded, or encoded only in transliteration without line-level tagging, are absent.
  6. Single archive. The Umma corpus is one node in a multi-archive system. Cross-archive analysis (adding Drehem, Girsu, Nippur) would substantially change the network picture.

Priorities for future work include: (1) manual validation of a 50-tablet stratified sample to estimate precision and recall; (2) implementation of ATF genre detection; (3) expansion to the Drehem subcorpus to test whether Drehem serves as the supply node for Umma relay brokers; (4) cross-referencing named individuals against the BDTNS prosopographic database to resolve name disambiguation and identify career trajectories.

6. Conclusion

A computational ATF-parsing pipeline applied to ~940 Ur III tablets from the Umma provincial archive has extracted 925 barley transactions involving 345 named actors. The principal findings are:

  1. A stable relay-broker cohort of 54 named officials mediates between institutional grain sources and downstream consumers, partially validating models of mid-level administrative agency in the Ur III redistributive economy.
  2. Bilateral documentation is elevated during campaign years (42% vs 27%), suggesting that military mobilisation periods imposed tighter administrative accountability, even though no military personnel appear as direct transaction parties.
  3. Seasonal patterns are consistent with the Umma agricultural calendar, with a harvest-month peak in sze-sag₁₁-ku₅ and an autumn secondary peak, lending indirect validation to the pipeline's extraction logic.
  4. No military logistics signal is present in this corpus. The Umma archive records grain flowing through civil redistributive channels; campaign references are calendar metadata, not logistics entries.

These findings are subject to the methodological limitations described in §5, foremost among them the absence of precision/recall validation and the 73% single-party coverage rate. They should be treated as hypotheses to be tested against philological and prosopographic controls rather than as settled conclusions. With those caveats in place, the pipeline demonstrates that computational analysis at the subcorpus scale can surface economically meaningful aggregate patterns invisible to traditional tablet-by-tablet study, and that the Ur III Umma archive is a productive testbed for developing and refining such methods.
